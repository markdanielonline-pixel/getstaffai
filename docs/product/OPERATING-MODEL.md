# Staff AI operating model

Mark's description, 2026-09-06. This is the canonical account of how the company
is meant to work. Where the product disagrees with this document, the product is
wrong.

## What we sell

Results, not features and not technology. Staff AI replaces the stack a small
business would otherwise assemble from HighLevel, HubSpot, ClickFunnels, Dubsado
and the rest. The customer does not configure any of it. They talk to their
Executive Assistant and the work happens.

Every product decision follows from that. If a screen exists because software
usually has that screen, it is a candidate for deletion. If a customer has to
understand a tool to get a result, we have failed.

## The two roles that matter

**The Executive Assistant is the CEO's only point of contact.** Nobody else
speaks to the CEO. The EA has two jobs.

First, it does the CEO's own work directly: business correspondence, drafting,
research, admin, keeping track of what the CEO owes people. It simplifies the
CEO's business life. Later it gains a wallet so the CEO's business bills are
paid on time without the CEO thinking about it.

Second, it is the interface to the company. Anything that is about the business
rather than about the CEO personally goes to the General Manager.

**The General Manager runs the business.** It manages the specialists, decides
who does what, plans when planning is needed, and reports upward. The GM never
speaks to the CEO directly. It reports to the EA, and the EA speaks to the CEO.

Both roles are load-bearing. The EA without the GM is a chatbot with no company
behind it. The GM without the EA is a manager the owner cannot reach.

## How a request travels

The CEO tells the EA what they want. It may be concrete ("email client four
today") or it may be an outcome ("we need sales to improve this month").

The EA passes anything business-shaped to the GM.

If the task is straightforward, the GM delegates it to the right specialist and
it gets done.

If the task needs planning, the GM plans it and sends a proposal back to the EA.
The EA puts the proposal to the CEO in plain language and asks for approval.

If the CEO approves, the GM executes. If the CEO has already given the team
standing authority for work of that kind, the GM proceeds without asking. That
authority is set by the CEO, not assumed by us.

Results travel back the same way: specialist to GM, GM to EA, EA to CEO.

## Reporting

The EA gives the CEO a daily report and a weekly report. The content comes from
the GM, who knows what the company did. The voice is the EA's, because the EA is
who the CEO talks to. The CEO should never receive a report from someone they do
not have a relationship with.

## What this means for the product

- One conversation. The CEO talks to the EA. Direct chat with individual
  employees is removed.
- The workforce view becomes a status view: who is employed, what each is
  working on, what is waiting on the CEO. Not a place to chat.
- Employees must be able to delegate to each other. Without that the EA cannot
  be a single point of contact, and the model above cannot exist. This is the
  spine, not a feature.
- Approvals must be real. A plan waiting on the CEO is a first-class object, not
  a message that scrolls away.
- The EA is the bottleneck by design, so the GM has to be able to carry on when
  the EA is restarting, and dispatched work has to survive both.

## Where this is going

Stated so that today's decisions do not close the door on it.

An EA tier with a photoreal interactive avatar that speaks with the CEO live and
attends virtual meetings with them or on their behalf, acting as an actual
company executive. The EA becomes a person the CEO works with rather than a
window they type into.

The practical consequence for now: the EA's identity, memory and relationship
with the CEO are product surface, not implementation detail. Its name, its
continuity across conversations, and its knowledge of the CEO should be built as
though someone will one day put a face on it.
